Phases of the Moon

The eight phases, drawn with the same glyph the app puts on the tide card. The lit limb is on the right while the Moon waxes and on the left while it wanes — the northern-hemisphere view.

Names in Irish

Gealach
the Moon (itself the app's name).
Gealach nua
new moon.
Gealach ag fás
a waxing moon — also gealach gheal, "bright moon".
Céad cheathrú
first quarter.
Gealach lán
full moon — lán is "full".
Gealach ag dul i laige
a waning moon — "the Moon fading".
An cheathrú dheireanach
last quarter.

Notes on the drawing

A phase is not a shape but a position: the Moon is always half-lit by the Sun, and what changes is how much of that lit half faces us. The glyph here is the app's — a disc of var(--moon) with the lit portion in var(--moon-lit), cut by a shadow circle of the same radius whose centre slides across, so the terminator is an ellipse rather than a straight line except exactly at the quarters.

One lunar month — new to new — is 29.53 days (the synodic month). The app derives the phase from that interval as an approximation: enough to name the phase and to find the next full and new moon, but not an ephemeris and carrying no positional accuracy. The "day" figures on each card are the point in that month at which the phase begins.

The first and last quarters are easy to confuse because both are half lit: waxing (first quarter) is lit on the right in the northern hemisphere, waning (last quarter) on the left.
