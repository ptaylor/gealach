Moon colours — a study

Candidate colour pairings for the Moon's disc, drawn with the app's own moonGlyph(). Each pairing is shown in both palettes, because a colour that works on a dark card can vanish on a white one. The contrast figure is the ratio between the lit limb and the unlit disc — the number that decides whether a crescent reads as a crescent or as a smudge.

Dark mode

How these were measured

Contrast is the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance ratio, computed with the standard sRGB transfer function. It is a proxy, not a law: nothing here is text, so the 4.5:1 text threshold does not apply. What matters is whether the lit and unlit halves separate — a ratio under about 1.5:1 reads as one flat circle at phone size, and the crescent phases are the first to fail, because a sliver has little area to carry the difference.

The bands used below: poor under 1.5:1, weak 1.5–2.0:1, good 2.0:1 and up. These are judgement calls for this particular drawing, not a standard.

One trap this page makes visible: --moon-lit is not only the Moon's lit limb. It also colours the Yeats quotes in the footer, the about panel and the Secret Rose overlay, and the saved-heart icon. A deeper gold to fix the disc therefore darkens that text too — which is why every swatch reports a second figure, the lit colour against the card, the one that governs whether the quotes still read as gold.
